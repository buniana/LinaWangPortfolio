Chatbot Design

Crisis Hotline

A mental health product can't only design for ordinary days. When someone feels completely overwhelmed, reaching out is hardest and matters most, so the crisis hotline had to work in a single, obvious step.

“During panic, a single obvious action beats many choices.”
Step 1

Alert screen

The app initiates a personalized well-being check.

Users can immediately call the hotline with a tap-and-hold action or dismiss with “I am okay.”

Step 2

Help recommendation

Based on responses, the app gently recommends professional support.

Users may choose to “Get help now” or “Contact later,” ensuring autonomy.

Three principles
Immediate responseUsers can reach help the first moment they need it.
Autonomous choiceWhen users aren't ready, clear paths let them get support later.
Error-tolerant designEvery step leaves room for correction, so a mis-tap never blocks help.
Step 3

Support options

Additional hotlines are presented with both call and text options.

A Back to Home action allows users to exit when ready.

Provider Dashboard

Clinicians follow their patients' progress, but they aren't trained to parse dense numbers quickly, and raw scores slowed their decisions. My first version leaned on detailed charts; I simplified it to a few key metrics, trends and color cues.

Solution

One screen to see who needs attention

Problem

Dense numbers slowed care

  • Providers aren’t trained to parse dense quant data fast
  • Raw scores slow decision-making
Impact

Faster first-read comprehension

Fewer clarifying clicks

Higher confidence in patient status at a glance

Rule 01

“At-a-glance” summaries first, drill-down second

Rule 02

Avoid heavy tables

Rule 03

Trend-based cues for EPDS
